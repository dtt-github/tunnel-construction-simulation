Tunnel Construction Simulation

An open simulator for mechanised tunnelling: published analytical models in the browser, GPU ground physics on the server.

Urban EPB shield (7 m class)

An earth-pressure-balance machine laid out front to back from published references, driven by the analytical models: chamber pressure held inside the DAUB support window by the screw conveyor, foam conditioning, thrust and torque breakdown, ring building with a 6 + key universal ring, and surface settlement under buildings. Shift pattern, downtime and tool interventions give a 90-day advance forecast, checked against published record rates.

Urban slurry shield (Mixshield)

A 7.08 m Crossrail-class Mixshield in saturated sand and gravel: submerged wall and air cushion holding the support pressure within ±10 kPa, feed and discharge lines with a crusher at the suction, filter cake or slurry penetration at the face, and the dry-mass balance and deposit velocity in the circuit.

Recorded GPU runs

Phase 0 benchmarks of the material-point method, recorded on the GPU server and replayed in the browser: a model tunnel face collapsing as its support retracts, and an EPB chamber stirred by a rotating cutterhead. They test speed and stability, not calibrated machine performance.

Hard-rock open gripper TBM

A 9.58 m main-beam TBM with the Gotthard Base Tunnel machine data: disc cutterhead with buckets, grippers and the regrip cycle, rock bolts, mesh and ring beams installed by rock class, shotcrete on the backup, and a fault zone that slows the drive.

Drill & blast road tunnel

A Norwegian T10.5 road tunnel driven round by round: the drilling pattern from published rules, a three-boom jumbo, charging, blast, ventilation, scaling, loader and trucks, bolts and sprayed concrete by support class, and vibration at a house above.

SEM / NATM heading

The Crossrail Farringdon platform tunnel in real London ground: an 11 m sprayed-concrete enlargement of a TBM pilot in Lambeth Group and London Clay, ring closure behind the face, spiles in a water-bearing sand lens, and predicted crown settlement (about 11 mm in the Lambeth Group) beside the measured 10–16.5 mm. A weak-rock heading with a fault zone stays selectable.

Hard-rock double shield TBM

A 9.9 m double shield with the Follo Line machine data, its penetration and average advance checked against Follo: telescopic and gripper shields, segment rings built while boring with pea-gravel backfill, and single-shield mode through the fault zone.
