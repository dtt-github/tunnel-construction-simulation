Ground and surface

Model limits

  • Volume loss is the measured Crossrail value (Ground panel), raised where London Clay is at the axis when the face pressure drops, by Macklin's (1999) load-factor relation; it never falls below the measured value and does not respond to grouting. Undrained strength is the Crossrail Paddington design profile carried along the line. One bore is modelled.
  • Buildings: OS OpenMap Local footprints (terraces merged into blocks), heights from Environment Agency LIDAR, listing from Historic England (all OGL). Damage uses the greenfield trough under each footprint, without building stiffness.
  • Building categories come from the second-stage assessment of Mair, Taylor & Burland (1996): the building follows the greenfield trough, and each hogging or sagging span's bending or diagonal strain (from Δ/L, as a masonry deep beam, E/G 2.6) is combined with its average horizontal strain. That is conservative: real buildings are stiffer. Each building also shows its Δ/L and largest horizontal strain with an estimated stiffness (Potts & Addenbrooke 1997, as plotted by Mair 1997; 1996, Fig. 5), from floor slabs every 3.4 m acting together (an assumed upper estimate). These are shown only; they do not change the category.
  • Downtime uses distributions fitted on one 6.88 m metro EPB (Tabriz; Moosazadeh et al. 2018) and a published shift pattern; they are illustrative for other projects. Locomotive travel time, learning curve and ground-related stops other than tool interventions are not modelled.
  • Chamber volume, screw efficiency, friction coefficients and machine weights are illustrative assumptions.
Models: wedge-silo face support and DAUB window, chamber volume balance with foam, simplified screw (Merritt & Mair), thrust and torque components (Shi et al. 2011), Gaussian settlement (Peck; O'Reilly & New; Mair & Taylor), utilisation and downtime (Moosazadeh et al. 2018; record rates with sources). Machine class from published references.
