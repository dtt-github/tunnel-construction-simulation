Ground and surface

Model limits

  • Volume loss is a scenario input (default from measured Crossrail settlement, see the Ground panel); it does not yet respond to face pressure or grouting. One bore is modelled.
  • Buildings: OS OpenMap Local footprints (terraces merged into blocks), heights from Environment Agency LIDAR, listing from Historic England (all OGL). Damage uses the greenfield trough under each footprint, without building stiffness.
  • Building categories come from the second-stage assessment of Mair, Taylor & Burland (1996): the building follows the greenfield trough, and each hogging or sagging span's bending or diagonal strain (from Δ/L, as a masonry deep beam, E/G 2.6) is combined with its average horizontal strain. That is conservative: real buildings are stiffer. Each building also shows its Δ/L and largest horizontal strain with an estimated stiffness (Potts & Addenbrooke 1997, as plotted by Mair 1997; 1996, Fig. 5), from floor slabs every 3.4 m acting together (an assumed upper estimate). These are shown only; they do not change the category.
  • The air-cushion dynamics (Boyle's law, level balance, first-order air regulator) are this project's own derivation; no open source with equations was found. Chamber area, air volume and regulator time are assumed.
  • Penetration uses DAUB's steady emax (conservative); the support transferred is approximated as 1 − e/L over the wedge length. Over-excavation when support falls short is illustrative.
  • The dry-mass balance assumes ideal density and flow meters; the separation plant is shown only as a load, not modelled.
  • Downtime uses distributions fitted on one 6.88 m metro EPB (Tabriz; Moosazadeh et al. 2018), applied to this slurry machine as an assumption. Separation-plant stops, pipe extensions and learning curve are not modelled.
  • Shield length, chamber sizes, friction coefficients and machine weights are illustrative assumptions.
Models: wedge-silo face support and DAUB window, filter cake and penetration (DAUB 2016; Broere & van Tol 2000), air cushion (own derivation), dry-mass balance (Shomee et al. 2022), deposit limit (Durand, after Miedema), thrust and torque components (Shi et al. 2011), Gaussian settlement (Peck; O'Reilly & New; Mair & Taylor), utilisation and downtime (Moosazadeh et al. 2018; record rates with sources). Machine class from published references.
